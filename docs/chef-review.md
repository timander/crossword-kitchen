# Chef's Review: Crossword Kitchen Word Banks

_Reviewed from the point of view of an executive chef and culinary educator. Food-safety numbers checked against the current FDA Food Code and USDA consumer guidance._

## 1. Overall assessment

These banks are in good shape. The clues are short, the vocabulary is real kitchen vocabulary, and the split between high school and college levels is mostly right. I found no dangerous food-safety errors. The fixes fall into four small groups:

- **Two factual slips:** the "smallest measuring spoon" clue and the definition of pearled barley.
- **Clues that hint at another answer in the same puzzle:** for example, a _Giblets_ clue that names _gizzard_, and a _Myoglobin_ clue that names _dark meat_. In a crossword, that hands students a free answer.
- **One clue that gives away its own answer:** "half" in the _Halve_ clue.
- **A few loose wordings** that a sharp student could fairly argue with.

In total I made 11 edits across 8 banks. I also wrote three new banks, and every bank passes the automated checks.

## 2. Per-bank review

### Commercial Kitchen Basics (`adv-commercial-kitchen`): adjusted

| Word | Before                                                            | After                                                               | Why                                                                                                                                     |
| ---- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hood | Ventilation system above the line, with built-in fire suppression | Exhaust canopy over the line that pulls out smoke, heat, and grease | A hood is the exhaust canopy. The fire-suppression system (e.g., Ansul) is a separate system mounted in it, and not every hood has one. |

Left alone: _Sheet pan_ (a full sheet really is 18 × 26 in), _Sanitizer_ (wash, rinse, sanitize is the correct order), and _FIFO_ (the spelled-out phrase is fine for an abbreviation answer).

### Poultry (`adv-poultry`): adjusted

| Word      | Before                                                | After                                                           | Why                                                                                              |
| --------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Giblets   | Heart, gizzard, and liver packed inside a whole bird  | Bag of neck and edible organs often packed inside a whole bird  | The old clue named _Gizzard_, which is another answer in this puzzle. It also left out the neck. |
| Myoglobin | Oxygen-storing protein that gives dark meat its color | Oxygen-storing muscle protein that makes legs and thighs darker | The old clue named _Dark meat_, which is another answer in this puzzle.                          |

Left alone: _Broiler_ ("under 10 weeks" matches the USDA class), _Salmonella_ (165°F is correct for poultry in both the Food Code and USDA guidance), and _Supreme_, _Ballotine_, and _Galantine_ (all accurate).

### Sauces & Pastry (`adv-sauces-pastry`): adjusted

| Word   | Before                                          | After                                                                | Why                                                                                             |
| ------ | ----------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Slurry | Starch mixed into cold liquid before thickening | Starch stirred into cold liquid, then whisked into a simmering sauce | The old wording was unclear about what gets thickened. The new one describes the actual method. |

Left alone: _Roux_ as "equal weights" (the standard teaching ratio) and _Bloom_ (gelatin bloom is clearly what the clue means).

Gap to consider: _Tomato_ sauce, the fifth mother sauce, isn't in this bank. You could swap it in if you'd like all five mother sauces covered.

### Culinary Techniques & Knife Cuts (`adv-techniques`): adjusted

| Word   | Before                                                    | After                                                              | Why                                                                                                  |
| ------ | --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Braise | Sear, then cook slowly, partly covered in liquid          | Sear, then cook slowly, covered, in a small amount of liquid       | "Partly covered" could be read as a half-on lid. A braise is lidded, with the food partly submerged. |
| Temper | Slowly raise the temperature of eggs so they don't curdle | Slowly whisk hot liquid into eggs so they warm up without curdling | "Temperature" starts with the answer. The automated word bank test caught this after the review.     |

Left alone: the cut sizes (1/8-in brunoise and 1/8 × 1/8 × 2-in julienne match CIA standards), _Mirepoix_ 2:1:1, and _Blanch_. Shocking isn't always part of blanching, but it's how the term is taught at this level.

### Food Service Etiquette (`intro-food-service-etiquette`): appropriate, no changes

Left alone: _Left/Right_. Many modern restaurants serve plated food from the right, but the word "Traditionally" in both clues keeps them correct, and this is the classic rule students get tested on.

### Kitchen Basics (`intro-kitchen-basics`): adjusted

| Word     | Before                                           | After                                                             | Why                                                        |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Teaspoon | Smallest common measuring spoon, abbreviated tsp | Measuring spoon abbreviated tsp; three of them equal a tablespoon | Factually wrong: most sets include ½, ¼, and ⅛ tsp spoons. |

### Pasta, Grains & Legumes (`intro-pasta-grains-legumes`): adjusted

| Word   | Before                              | After                                            | Why                                                                                                         |
| ------ | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Barley | …"pearled" when its hull is removed | …"pearled" when polished to remove hull and bran | Barley with only the hull removed is _hulled_ barley. _Pearled_ barley is also polished to remove the bran. |

### Recipe Math & Measurement (`intro-recipe-math`): adjusted

| Word  | Before                                           | After                                                   | Why                                                                                                                               |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Halve | To cut a recipe down to half the original amount | To make a recipe 50% as big, divide every amount by two | "Half" was an obvious form of the answer.                                                                                         |
| Yield | The number of servings a recipe makes            | The total amount or number of servings a recipe makes   | Yield can also be a volume or weight (e.g., 2 quarts). This also stops the clue duplicating the Kitchen Basics one word for word. |

Left alone: all the equivalents. 3 tsp = 1 Tbsp, 16 Tbsp = 1 cup, 2 Tbsp = 1 fl oz, and about 28 g per oz are all correct.

### Food Safety & Nutrition (`intro-safety-nutrition`): adjusted

| Word            | Before                                                      | After                                                     | Why                                                                                                                |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| _(description)_ | Read each clue carefully, then write one letter per square. | Multi-word answers show their letter counts, e.g. (6, 4). | _Danger zone_ and _Grease fire_ are two-word answers, so the description now follows the style of the other banks. |

Left alone: _Danger zone_ at **40–140°F**. That is USDA consumer guidance and the right number for an intro class. The FDA Food Code, used in commercial kitchens and ServSafe, uses **41–135°F**. If students are preparing for a food handler card, tell them about both numbers. _Handwashing_ at 20 seconds is also left alone; it matches CDC guidance and the Food Code's total handwash time. This bank has 15 words, which is within the allowed range.

## 3. The three new banks

**`grad-culinary-arts`: Culinary Arts Graduates.** A capstone mix: costing (_Food cost_, _Yield test_, _Q factor_, _Breakeven_), menu engineering (_Plowhorse_, _Puzzle_), management (_Covers_, _HACCP_), classical cuisine (_Escoffier_, _Carême_, _Allemande_, _Gastrique_, _Duxelles_), and food science (_Lecithin_, _Retrogradation_, _Brix_). Students who have done a menu-engineering matrix will enjoy _Puzzle_ turning up as an answer in a crossword.

**`intro-fish-seafood`: Intro to Foods · High School.** Covers seafood categories (_Shellfish_, _Mollusk_, _Crustacean_), freshness checks (_Gills_, _Eyes_, _Smell_, _Mussels_ that close when tapped), safe handling (_Thaw_ in the fridge, doneness at 145°F with _Opaque_ flesh), cooking methods (_Poach_, _En papillote_), and good-citizen topics (_Mercury_, _Sustainable_). The _Mercury_ clue follows FDA/EPA advice about limiting high-mercury fish during pregnancy. It's worded neutrally, but read the room before you hand it out.

**`pro-michelin-kitchen`: Professional · Michelin-Star Kitchen.** Covers brigade roles (_Chef de partie_, _Commis_, _Saucier_, _Entremetier_, _Garde manger_), service language (_Oui chef_, _Fire_, _Hands_, _Stage_), technique (_Quenelle_, _Beurre noisette_, _Arroser_, _Glace de viande_), ingredients (_Ikejime_, _Bresse_), and plating (_Tweezers_). Service calls differ from kitchen to kitchen. _Hands_ is common in UK and US fine dining, and some kitchens say "Service!" instead. Accents are stripped from answers automatically.

## 4. Suggested new word bank topics

### High School intro

1. **Fruits & Vegetables.** Produce ID and handling is the base of every foods class.
   - `Ethylene: Gas from ripening bananas that speeds up ripening of nearby fruit`
   - `Blanch: Quick dip in boiling water before freezing vegetables`
   - `Cruciferous: Vegetable family that includes broccoli, cabbage, and kale`
   - `Oxidation: Reaction that turns cut apples brown`
2. **Eggs & Dairy.** Cheap to cook in class, and full of technique.
   - `Yolk: Yellow center of an egg, rich in fat`
   - `Pasteurize: Heat milk to kill harmful bacteria`
   - `Curdle: What milk does when it separates into lumps`
   - `Scramble: Stir eggs in a pan as they cook`
3. **Baking Basics.** Leaveners and mixing methods trip up beginners.
   - `Leavening: Ingredient like baking soda that makes batter rise`
   - `Gluten: Stretchy protein formed when flour is mixed with water`
   - `Cream: Beat butter and sugar together until light and fluffy`
   - `Level: Scrape across a measuring cup with a straight edge`
4. **Kitchen Safety & First Aid.** Cuts and burns are the most common injuries in a classroom kitchen.
   - `Claw: Hand grip that tucks fingertips away from the knife`
   - `Burn: Cool this injury under running water, not ice`
   - `Extinguisher: Remember PASS to use one: pull, aim, squeeze, sweep`
   - `Spill: Wipe this up right away to prevent slips`
5. **Global Breakfasts.** A friendly way into world food cultures.
   - `Congee: Rice porridge eaten across East and Southeast Asia`
   - `Shakshuka: Eggs poached in spiced tomato sauce`
   - `Chilaquiles: Fried tortilla pieces simmered in salsa`
   - `Crepe: Thin French pancake`

### College advanced

6. **Meat Fabrication.** Primal and subprimal cuts, and how to cook each.
   - `Primal: Large first cut of a carcass, like the chuck or loin`
   - `Silverskin: Tough connective tissue trimmed off a tenderloin`
   - `Marbling: Fat flecks within the lean that affect USDA grade`
   - `Chuck: Shoulder primal used for braises and ground beef`
7. **Stocks & Soups.** Ratios and clarification.
   - `Remouillage: Second, weaker stock made from bones already used once`
   - `Bisque: Rich, creamy shellfish soup`
   - `Sachet: Cheesecloth bag of herbs and spices for flavoring stock`
   - `Raft: Floating mass that clears a consommé`
8. **Garde Manger & Charcuterie.** Curing, forcemeats, and cold platters.
   - `Forcemeat: Emulsified mix of ground meat, fat, and seasoning`
   - `Terrine: Forcemeat cooked in a lidded mold`
   - `Pellicle: Tacky skin that forms on cured food before smoking`
   - `Nitrite: Curing salt that keeps meats pink and blocks botulism`
9. **Food Safety Management (ServSafe-level).** Commercial temperatures and Food Code rules.
   - `TCS: Food needing time and temperature control for safety (abbr.)`
   - `Cooling: Two-stage process: 135°F to 70°F in 2 hours, then to 41°F within 4 more`
   - `Norovirus: Highly contagious virus most often spread by ill food workers`
   - `Shellstock: Live shellfish whose tags must be kept for 90 days`
10. **Breads & Viennoiserie.** Fermentation and lamination.
    - `Poolish: Wet preferment of equal flour and water by weight`
    - `Autolyse: Rest after mixing just flour and water`
    - `Croissant: Laminated, crescent-shaped yeast pastry`
    - `Score: Slash dough so it expands evenly in the oven`

### Graduates / Professional

11. **Beverage & Wine Pairing.** Front-of-house knowledge expected at fine dining.
    - `Sommelier: Restaurant's wine professional`
    - `Tannin: Drying, astringent compound in red wine and tea`
    - `Corked: Wine with a musty TCA fault`
    - `Decant: Pour wine off its sediment into another vessel`
12. **Modernist Technique.** Hydrocolloids and precision cooking.
    - `Spherification: Forming liquid spheres with alginate and calcium`
    - `Agar: Seaweed gel that stays set at higher temperatures than gelatin`
    - `Xanthan: Gum that thickens with no heat at very small doses`
    - `Rotovap: Low-pressure distiller used to capture delicate aromas`
13. **Restaurant Operations & Finance.** Running the business side.
    - `Prime cost: Cost of goods sold plus total labor`
    - `Turnover: Rate at which inventory or staff is replaced`
    - `Comp: Remove an item from the bill as a goodwill gesture`
    - `Variance: Gap between theoretical and actual food usage`
14. **Fermentation & Preservation.** Koji, lacto-ferments, and curing in modern kitchens.
    - `Koji: Mold, Aspergillus oryzae, behind miso, soy sauce, and sake`
    - `Lactobacillus: Bacteria that sour sauerkraut and kimchi`
    - `Garum: Ancient Roman fermented fish sauce`
    - `Brine: Salt solution, often 2% for vegetable ferments`
